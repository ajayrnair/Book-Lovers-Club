Reading Peeps

For libraries and their book clubs

Reading Peeps is a free website that handles the logistics of a book club: choosing the next book, finding a meeting date, tracking reading progress, and sending reminders. It is built and run by one reader, Ajay Nair in Bothell, Washington, for his own clubs. It costs nothing, it shows no ads, and it will never charge.

Meet Reading Peeps, in 54 seconds.

The problem it solves

Many library book clubs are held together by one person: the librarian who leads the group, or the member who borrows a book kit and organizes everyone else. That person carries the next book vote, the meeting date, and the reminders in email threads and paper lists. Reading Peeps holds those pieces in one place, so the meeting itself can stay about the book and the people who came to talk about it.

How a club uses it

The organizer starts a club and invites the members. The club keeps a shared library, votes on the next book, and marks the days each person is free in the meeting week, and the organizer picks the day that fits the most people. Members track their progress and rate the book when they finish. Reminders go out by email and in the app. It runs in the browser on any phone or computer, so there is nothing to install.

What it is not

Reading Peeps has no chat, no feed, and nothing built to keep people scrolling. It is not a social network, and the discussion belongs in the room, at the meeting. It also has no ads and no payments, now or later.
